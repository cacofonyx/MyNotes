# A Call to Action — Conclusion to The DevOps Handbook

## The Core Argument

Left unmanaged, the inherent Dev/Ops conflict creates: slower time to market, poor quality, increased outages, growing technical debt, reduced productivity, employee dissatisfaction and burnout.

DevOps principles break this conflict — enabling fast flow, world-class reliability and security, competitiveness, and employee satisfaction.

## What DevOps Requires

- New cultural and management norms
- Changes in technical practices and architecture
- A coalition spanning: business leadership, Product Management, Development, QA, IT Operations, Information Security, and Marketing
- A safe system of work where small teams quickly and independently develop, validate, and deploy code

## The Honest Warning

> "In many of our case studies, following the achievement of the breakthrough results presented, many of the change agents were promoted — but, in some cases, there was later a change of leadership which resulted in many of the people involved leaving, accompanied by a rolling back of the organizational changes they had created."

The authors' response: don't be cynical. The change agents knew failure was possible and did it anyway. They inspired others by showing what's possible.

> "Don't fight stupid, make more awesome." — Jesse Robbins

## The Promise

DevOps brings joy back to developing products. Fewer death marches. Humane work conditions. Fewer weekends and missed holidays. Teams working together to survive, learn, thrive, delight customers, and help the organization succeed.

> **[SRE Lens]** The call to action is also a warning for SRE leaders: transformation gains are fragile. A leadership change can roll back years of progress. The defense against this is making the practices systemic — embedded in tooling, processes, career ladders, and culture — not dependent on any single champion. If your SRE practices collapse when you leave, they weren't practices. They were personal habits. Build institutions, not dependencies.
