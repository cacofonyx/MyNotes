# One More Thing... (Epilogue)

> **High Output Management** — Andrew S. Grove
> *Grove's Action Assignments — The Book Distilled into Practice*

Grove closes with a characteristic move: he doesn't ask you to *agree* with him or *feel inspired* by him. He asks you to **do something.** The epilogue is a scored checklist of specific assignments — each tied to a concept from the book — that translate reading into practice. This is Grove's leverage equation applied to the reader: the book cost 8 hours to read; the assignments will produce orders of magnitude more output if actually performed.

> *"If you do at least 100 points worth of what you find here, you'll be a distinctly better manager for it."*

---

## The Assignments

Grove groups them by theme and assigns point values (10-20 points each). Here they are, organized by book concept:

### Production (Chapters 1-2)

| Points | Assignment | Book Concept |
|--------|-----------|-------------|
| 10 | Identify the process, assembly, and test operations in your work | Three production operations |
| 10 | For a current project, identify the limiting step and map the flow around it | Limiting step + time offsets |
| 10 | Define receiving, in-process, and final inspection equivalents. Decide gate vs. monitor. Identify conditions for variable inspection. | Quality inspection hierarchy |
| 10 | Identify six new output indicators for your group (quantity AND quality) | Paired indicators |
| 20 | Install these indicators and review them regularly in staff meetings | Indicators in practice |
| 20 | Describe your most important strategy: the environmental demand, your status, and whether it will close the gap | Three-step planning |

### Leverage (Chapters 3-6)

| Points | Assignment | Book Concept |
|--------|-----------|-------------|
| 10 | Conduct work simplification on your most tedious task. Eliminate 30%+ of steps. | Work simplification |
| 10 | Define your output: list the output elements of your org and orgs you influence, in order of importance | Manager's output equation |
| 10 | Analyze your information-gathering system: balance of headlines, articles, magazines? Redundancy? | Information hierarchy |
| 10 | Take a "tour" — visit an area you normally don't. List transactions you got involved in. | Walking around |
| 10 | Create a monthly "excuse" for a tour | Regularity principle |
| 10 | Describe how you'll monitor the next delegated project: what, how, how often? | Delegation + monitoring |
| 10 | Generate an inventory of discretionary projects for slack time | Project inventory |
| 20 | Hold a scheduled 1-1 with each subordinate (prepare them in advance) | 1-1 meetings |
| 10 | Classify last week's activities as low/medium/high leverage. Plan to do more high-leverage. | Leverage audit |
| 10 | Forecast next week's time. What % in meetings? Process vs. mission? If mission >25%, reduce. | Calendar as production tool |
| 20 | Define three most important objectives for your org for next three months with key results | MBO/OKR |
| 20 | Have subordinates do the same, after discussing your objectives | Cascading OKRs |
| 10 | Generate inventory of pending decisions. Structure three using the six-question approach. | Decision-making framework |

### Performance (Chapters 11-16)

| Points | Assignment | Book Concept |
|--------|-----------|-------------|
| 10 | Evaluate your own motivational state on Maslow's hierarchy. Do the same for each subordinate. | Maslow's hierarchy |
| 20 | Give subordinates a racetrack: define performance indicators for each | Sports analogy |
| 10 | List the forms of task-relevant feedback your subordinates receive. How well can they gauge progress? | Task-relevant feedback |
| 10 | Classify each subordinate's TRM as low/medium/high. Match your management style. Compare actual vs. ideal. | Task-relevant maturity |
| 20 | Evaluate the last performance review you received and the last ones you gave. How well did they improve performance? | Performance appraisal |
| 10 | Redo one review as it should have been done | Deliberate practice |

> **[Senior EM Application: The 100-Point Challenge]**
>
> Grove says 100 points makes you "a distinctly better manager." Here's a curated 100-point path for a Senior EM in SRE:
>
> | Assignment | Points | Why This First |
> |-----------|--------|---------------|
> | Define your output equation (your org + influenced orgs) | 10 | Foundation — you can't improve what you haven't defined |
> | Classify last week as low/medium/high leverage | 10 | Awareness — see where your time actually goes |
> | Define 3 quarterly objectives with key results | 20 | Alignment — your teams need direction |
> | Hold scheduled 1-1s with all reports | 20 | Relationship — the highest-leverage regular meeting |
> | Give each subordinate a racetrack (performance indicators) | 20 | Motivation — create self-sustaining drive |
> | Classify each subordinate's TRM and match your style | 10 | Effectiveness — stop managing everyone the same way |
> | Redo one performance review properly | 10 | Skill — practice the hardest, highest-leverage task |
> | **Total** | **100** | |
>
> Do these seven things — genuinely, not as a checkbox exercise — and Grove guarantees you'll be a better manager. The rest of the assignments extend the improvement, but these seven cover the essentials: know your output, manage your time, set direction, build relationships, create motivation, calibrate your style, and give meaningful feedback.

> **[Production Thinking: The Epilogue as Quality Gate]**
>
> The epilogue is Grove being consistent to the end. In Chapter 1, he said: *"detect problems at the lowest-value stage."* In Chapter 2: *"output, not activity, is what counts."* In Chapter 3: *"the output of planning is decisions made and actions taken."*
>
> The book itself is a production process. The raw material was your time (8 hours). The process was reading. The output is not "knowledge of management concepts" (activity) but **changed behavior** (output). The epilogue is the final inspection — it tests whether the process produced actual output or just passed through without effect.
>
> If you read the book and do nothing differently, the production process failed. If you do 100 points of assignments, the process succeeded. Grove, characteristically, gives you the quality gate *and* the measurement system. The rest is up to you.

## Book Complete

The full arc of *High Output Management*:

```
PART I: PRODUCTION (Ch 1-2)
  How does work flow? How do you measure and control it?

PART II: MANAGEMENT LEVERAGE (Ch 3-6)
  What is a manager's output? How do you maximize it?
  Meetings as medium. Decisions as output. Planning as today's action for tomorrow.

PART III: TEAM OF TEAMS (Ch 7-10)
  How should organizations be structured?
  Hybrid orgs. Dual reporting. Modes of control.

PART IV: THE PLAYERS (Ch 11-16)
  How do individuals perform at their best?
  Motivation. Task-relevant maturity. Performance reviews.
  Interviewing. Compensation. Training.

EPILOGUE: Now go do something.
```

Every chapter builds on the previous ones. Production principles (Part I) become managerial principles (Part II) which shape organizational design (Part III) which creates the context for individual performance (Part IV). And the manager's job — from the first page to the last — is to maximize the output of their organization through leverage, feedback, and the relentless pursuit of high performance.

> *"The output of a manager is the output of the organizational units under his or her supervision or influence."*
